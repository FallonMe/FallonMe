# Fang (Fallon) Meng

**Computational biologist · Population and evolutionary genomics · Molecular and cardiovascular biology**

I came to genomics through the bench. Before my PhD, I worked in stem-cell and cardiovascular biology, molecular research, and pharmaceutical microbiology. That training still shapes how I approach computational work: I want to connect sequence and expression patterns to biological mechanisms, phenotypes, and experimental evidence.

I am a PhD candidate in Biology at New York University, where I study how urbanization and environmental change shape biological invasion. My current work combines population and comparative genomics, statistical genetics, demographic inference, transcriptomics, and quantitative physiology. I am looking for postdoctoral and computational biology, bioinformatics, or genomics scientist roles.

## Research path

- **Urban and evolutionary genomics — NYU (2022–present):** I use whole-genome data to study spotted-lanternfly invasion history, population structure, urban–rural differentiation, and candidate genomic responses to environmental pressures. I am first author of the 2026 *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* study on cities as evolutionary incubators, and co-first author of the chromosome-level reference genome paper. [Population-genomics code, metadata, and reproducibility notes](https://github.com/FallonMe/SLF-Pop-Gen-MS) · [Genome assembly fork](https://github.com/FallonMe/SLF-Genome-Assembly).
- **Stem-cell and cardiovascular biology — The Chinese University of Hong Kong (2019–2022):** I studied cardiomyocyte differentiation, maturation, regeneration, and metabolic remodeling using molecular and cell biology, hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, imaging, animal models, and metabolic assays. This work led to a first-author paper on cardiomyocyte plasticity and an inventor role on a pending U.S. patent application.
- **Pharmaceutical microbiology — Bright Future Pharmaceutical Laboratories (2018–2019):** I performed microbiological quality-control and analytical testing, including microbial-limit assays, organism identification and purity checks, environmental monitoring, antibiotic bioassays, and culture-media suitability testing. I reviewed records and prepared reports supporting product release in a regulated laboratory.
- **Earlier molecular research:** My undergraduate and master’s projects included dsRNA-vector construction, RNA and protein work, PCR/RT-PCR, western blotting, bacterial assays, and analytical chemistry.

## Methods & tools

- **Computational genomics:** comparative and population genomics; phylogenomics; statistical genetics; genomic alignment and variant calling; genome assembly and annotation; population structure; demographic inference; selection scans; landscape genomics.
- **Transcriptomics and statistics:** bulk RNA-seq; differential gene expression; coexpression networks; transcript usage and alternative splicing; statistical modeling; integration of genomic, environmental, and phenotypic datasets; quality control and reproducible analysis.
- **Programming and computing:** R, Python, Bash, Linux/Unix, and high-performance computing; GATK, ANGSD, DREAM, WGCNA, and rMATS; reproducible scripts, workflow organization, and research data documentation.
- **Molecular and experimental biology:** stem-cell and hiPSC-derived cardiomyocyte culture and differentiation; primary cell culture; RNA/protein extraction; PCR and RT-PCR; western blotting; microscopy; cell-based and metabolic assays; animal models; virology; bacterial culture and bioassays.
- **Pharmaceutical laboratory practice:** microbial-limit and purity testing; microbial identification; environmental monitoring; antibiotic bioassays; culture-media suitability; analytical equipment calibration; controlled records and product-release reporting.
